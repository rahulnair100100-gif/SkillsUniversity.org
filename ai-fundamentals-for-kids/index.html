Every parent is asking the same three things

🤷

"They're already using it — I just don't know how."

Homework, games, group chats. It is already in their life, usually without a single rule around it.

📝

"Is it doing their thinking for them?"

The difference between a tutor and a shortcut is a skill. Nobody is teaching it at school yet.

🛡️

"How do I know what's actually safe?"

Fake images, made-up facts, data they should never share. They need to spot all three themselves.

The curriculum

Six modules, in the right order

Each module builds on the last: understand it, talk to it, learn with it, create with it, build with it, then question it.

01

What AI actually is

The demystifying module. How a machine learns from examples, why it makes mistakes, and where AI is already hiding in their day.

  • Learning from data, explained simply
  • AI vs. a normal computer program
  • Where AI already lives: maps, feeds, games
  • Why AI gets things confidently wrong
02

Talking to AI properly

Most kids type six words and accept whatever comes back. This module teaches them to ask like someone who knows what they want.

  • Giving context, role and format
  • Following up instead of restarting
  • Getting AI to explain its reasoning
  • Knowing when the answer is nonsense
03

Learning with AI, not cheating with it

The module parents care about most. Using AI as a patient tutor rather than a homework machine — and knowing exactly where the line is.

  • Turning AI into a study partner
  • Fact-checking before trusting
  • Academic honesty in plain language
  • Revision techniques that actually work
04

Creating with AI

The fun one. Images, stories, music and video — plus how to keep their own voice instead of sounding like everyone else's chatbot.

  • AI images and illustration
  • Story and comic writing
  • Music and voice basics
  • Making it theirs, not the machine's
05

Building a first project

From consumer to creator. Your child ships something real — with AI as the assistant and them as the one making the decisions.

  • Planning a small project properly
  • Building with no-code AI tools
  • Testing, fixing and improving it
  • Sharing it safely with family
06

Staying safe & thinking critically

The module we would make compulsory if we could. What never to share, how to spot a fake, and how to stay the sceptic in the room.

  • Personal data: what never goes in a chat
  • Spotting deepfakes and fake images
  • Misinformation and AI bias
  • Healthy habits and screen limits

Safety & responsibility

Designed for parents to feel comfortable saying yes

We treat safety as curriculum, not a disclaimer. Here is exactly how this course handles it.

✓
Parent-purchased, parent-supervised. The account is yours. Any tool sign-ups are done by an adult.
✓
Age-appropriate tools only. We demonstrate mainstream tools that are suitable for this age group with adult set-up.
✓
A full module on safety. Personal data, deepfakes, misinformation and bias — taught, not mentioned.
✓
Honesty is taught explicitly. Module 3 is built around using AI to learn rather than to avoid learning.
✓
No live chat, no community. There is no forum and no messaging — nothing for a stranger to reach your child through.
✓
Watch it yourself first. You have full access to every module, so you can preview anything before they see it.
Is this right for my child?

This course is built for them if…

✓
They are between 10 and 16 and already curious about AI — or already using it without much guidance.
✓
You would rather they learn the rules from a structured course than from a friend at school or a YouTube short.
✓
You want screen time that produces something — a story, a project, a skill — rather than another hour of scrolling.
