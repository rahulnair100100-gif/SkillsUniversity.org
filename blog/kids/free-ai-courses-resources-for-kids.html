Free AI Courses & Resources for Kids (2026): The Complete List

You don't need to spend a penny to get your child started with AI. Here are the genuinely free tools and courses worth using — and an honest note on when a paid course earns its place.

SkillsUniversity Team • September 17, 2026 • 8 min read

Search "AI for kids" and you'll hit a wall of expensive bootcamps. Here's what the ads won't tell you: some of the best starting points cost nothing. This is a curated, honestly-reviewed list of free AI resources for children aged roughly 8–16 — the ones actually worth your child's time — plus a straight answer on when free stops being enough.

The short answer
  • Yes — excellent free AI resources exist, and they're the right place to start if your child has never touched AI.
  • Best free tools: Google Teachable Machine, Quick, Draw!, AI Experiments, Scratch, Chrome Music Lab.
  • Best free courses: Code.org's AI content, MIT's Day of AI, Google's AI education videos, Common Sense Media's AI-literacy lessons.
  • Free tools spark interest; a structured course adds sequence, safety and follow-through. Start free, upgrade when your child is hooked.

Are there really free AI courses for kids?

Yes. Big organisations — Google, MIT, Code.org and Common Sense Media among them — publish free, high-quality AI material for children and families. The catch isn't quality; it's structure. Free resources tend to be scattered: a tool here, a video there, a lesson plan built for a classroom. For a curious child with an involved parent, that's often plenty to begin. What free rarely gives you is a clear path from "what is AI?" to "I built something and I know how to stay safe."

Teachable Machine is the single best free "aha" moment. In ten minutes a child trains a model to tell their face from yours — and instantly understands that AI "learns from examples" instead of being magic.

Best free AI courses and lessons

  • Code.org — AI content & "AI for Oceans." Short, classroom-friendly activities that explain how AI learns and why ethics matter.
  • MIT RAISE — Day of AI. Research-backed lesson plans a parent can run at home; strong on how AI works and its limits.
  • Google — AI education videos. Short explainers and Teachable Machine tutorials, good for shorter attention spans.
  • Common Sense Media — AI literacy lessons. Parent-trusted, with a strong emphasis on safety and responsible use.
  • CS Unplugged. Screen-free activities that teach the thinking behind AI using cards and games — ideal if you want to cut screen time.

A free 3-phase starter plan

Don't hand your child a list of ten links. Use this sequence over a few weekends:

  1. Phase 1 — Spark (one afternoon). Play Quick, Draw! and one or two AI Experiments. The goal is curiosity, not mastery.
  2. Phase 2 — Understand (a weekend). Train a model in Teachable Machine and watch one Code.org or Day of AI lesson together. Now "learning from data" is real, not abstract.
  3. Phase 3 — Create (ongoing). Build a small Scratch project or make music in Chrome Music Lab. Your child moves from *using* AI to *making* something with it.

How to keep free tools safe

  • Check age rules: general assistants like ChatGPT and Gemini set a 13+ minimum, and under-13s should use kid-focused or parent-supervised tools.
  • An adult should handle any account setup and sign-ins.
  • Sit in for the first session of any new tool — you'll spot anything odd and share the fun.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free AI tool for kids?

For a first "how does AI actually work?" moment, Google Teachable Machine is hard to beat — a child trains a working model in minutes with no code. For younger children, Quick, Draw! is the friendliest entry point.

Are there free AI courses for kids under 10?

Yes — Code.org activities, Quick, Draw! and Scratch all suit under-10s with a parent nearby. Full "courses" tend to be pitched at 10+, which is also the age most structured programmes (including ours) begin.

Is free AI enough, or do I need to pay?

Free is the ideal start and may be all you need for a casual explorer. Pay when you want structure, safety and prompting taught properly, and a clear path your child won't stall on — see our free-vs-paid comparison above.

Are free AI tools safe for children?

The reputable ones (Teachable Machine, Scratch, Code.org) are designed for education and are safe with normal supervision. Avoid open-ended companion chatbots for kids, and have an adult handle any sign-ups.

What order should my child try free AI resources in?

Spark first (playful demos), then understand (train a model, watch one lesson), then create (a small project). Our 3-phase plan above lays it out.
