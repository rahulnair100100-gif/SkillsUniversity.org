ChatGPT & Kids

Should My Child Use ChatGPT?

One of the most-searched parenting questions of 2026 — answered honestly, without the fear-mongering, for ages 10 to 16.

SkillsUniversity Team • August 8, 2026 • 7 min read

It's one of the most-searched parenting questions of 2026, and for good reason: ChatGPT is everywhere your child is — in homework apps, on YouTube, in classroom conversations. So the honest question isn't really "should my child use ChatGPT?" It's "how do I make sure they use it well?" Here's a clear, no-fear-mongering guide.

The short answer

For most children aged 10–16, supervised, learning-focused use of ChatGPT can be genuinely beneficial — once a few rules are in place around homework honesty, privacy, and checking answers. For under-13s, an adult should set up and oversee any use, and general chatbots aren't designed for unsupervised use by young children.

The honest answer: it depends on age and setup

There's no one-size answer, because a supervised 15-year-old using ChatGPT to explain a physics concept is in a completely different situation from a 9-year-old left alone with it. What stays constant across the 10–16 range: an adult sets it up, rules are agreed in advance, and the child learns to treat AI as a tool to think with, not a machine to think for them.

Your child's classmates are almost certainly using it already — surveys through 2025 and 2026 show a large majority of teens have used a generative AI tool. So "just ban it" rarely works; it usually just pushes use underground, where there's no guidance at all.

Where ChatGPT genuinely helps a child

  • Explaining hard concepts in simpler words, from a different angle than the textbook.
  • Being a patient study partner — generating practice questions, quizzing them, checking their reasoning.
  • Sparking creativity — brainstorming stories, exploring "what if" questions, learning to prompt.
  • Building a modern skill — learning to use AI well is fast becoming as basic as searching the web.

The real risks (and how to handle them)

Three concerns are worth genuine attention, and all three are manageable:

  • Homework shortcuts. The line: using AI to understand is good; using it to produce work they hand in as their own isn't. Teach the difference explicitly.
  • Confident wrong answers. ChatGPT can state false things convincingly. Teach your child to verify anything important — a habit that protects them everywhere.
  • Privacy. Set an early rule: no full names, address, school, passwords, or photos ever go into a chat.
A simple test your child can remember

Before using AI on schoolwork, ask: did it help me think, or did it think for me? The first builds understanding they keep. The second leaves them with nothing when the exam comes and there's no AI in the room.

How to set it up safely

  1. You own the account. An adult sets it up, on an adult's email.
  2. Agree the rules together before first use — honesty, privacy, fact-checking.
  3. Start alongside them. Sit in for the first few sessions so you can guide.
  4. Check in, don't spy. Ask what they made and learned, keep it a conversation.

For younger children specifically, look at purpose-built educational AI tools rather than general chatbots — they're designed with children in mind and give you more oversight.

The bottom line

Should your child use ChatGPT? For most 10–16 year olds, yes — supervised, with clear rules, and taught as a tool to learn with rather than a shortcut around learning. The children who benefit are the ones whose parents stayed involved and set the guardrails early. That guidance, not a ban, is what turns AI from a risk into a genuine advantage.

This article is general guidance for parents, not a substitute for your own judgment about your individual child and the specific tools they use.
