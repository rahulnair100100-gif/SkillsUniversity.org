Homework & Honesty

Stop Your Child Cheating With AI

The homework got suspiciously good overnight? Here's the fix that works better than banning everything.

SkillsUniversity Team • July 24, 2026 • 6 min read

You suspect it. The homework got suddenly, suspiciously good. The writing does not sound like them. If you have searched "how to stop my child using ChatGPT to cheat," this is for you — and the answer is more hopeful than banning everything.

The short answer

You cannot reliably block AI, and trying mostly pushes it underground. The winning move is to teach the line between using AI as a tutor and using it as a shortcut — explicitly, with examples. A child who understands why copied work is worthless to them cheats far less than one who is merely forbidden.

Why banning does not work

Filters, VPN blocks and banned apps feel decisive, but they fail in practice. Detection tools for AI-written text are unreliable. Kids access AI on phones, friends' devices and school computers. And a household built on blocking teaches secrecy, not judgement — the child learns to hide the tool, not to use it well.

Around a quarter of teenagers already report using AI chatbots for schoolwork, and that number is climbing. The realistic goal is not zero AI. It is honest AI.

The line every child needs drawn for them

Most children have genuinely never had anyone explain where the line sits. It is not obvious. Here is the version that lands:

  • Tutor use (good): asking AI to explain a concept, quiz them, check their reasoning, or suggest how to improve something they wrote themselves.
  • Shortcut use (cheating): asking AI to produce the answer or the essay and handing it in as their own.

The test a child can remember: "Did the AI help me think, or did it think for me?" The first builds skill they keep. The second leaves them with nothing when the exam arrives and there is no AI in the room.

The point that changes behaviour

The most powerful thing you can tell your child is not “you'll get caught.” It is: work you did not do yourself is worth nothing to you. The grade is temporary; the understanding is what they actually keep. Once that clicks, honesty stops being a rule and becomes self-interest.

How to turn AI into a real tutor

Rather than fighting AI, redirect it. Show your child how to use it the way a good tutor works:

  1. Explain, don't answer. "Explain this using a simple real-life example," not "give me the answer."
  2. Quiz me. Turn AI into a patient examiner that generates practice questions on their actual topics.
  3. Fact-check first. Treat every AI claim as a draft to verify, not a truth to copy.
  4. Improve my own work. Write it themselves, then ask AI where it could be clearer or stronger.

Used this way, AI becomes what a good tutor is — infinitely patient, available at 8pm on a Tuesday, and focused on their understanding.

Signs to watch for (and how to respond)

Common tells that a child leaned on a shortcut: a sudden jump in ability, vocabulary or tone that is not theirs, or an inability to explain their own work. If you see them, the response is a conversation, not a confiscation. Ask them to walk you through how they got there. If they cannot, that is the teaching moment — not a punishment, but a chance to draw the line clearly.

The bottom line

You will not out-block AI, and you do not need to. Teach your child the difference between a tutor and a shortcut, help them feel why honest work matters to them, and show them how to make AI genuinely improve their learning.
