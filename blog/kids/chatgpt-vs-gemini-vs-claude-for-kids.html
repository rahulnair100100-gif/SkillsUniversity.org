ChatGPT vs Gemini vs Claude for Kids & Students: Which Is Best (2026)?

Three big AI assistants, one confused parent. Here's a straight comparison on safety, age limits, cost and what each is actually good at for a 10–16 year old.

SkillsUniversity Team • September 17, 2026 • 8 min read

ChatGPT, Google's Gemini and Anthropic's Claude are the three assistants your child is most likely to meet. Parents want a simple "use this one." The honest answer is that they're more alike than different, they all share the same 13+ age rule, and the thing that actually matters isn't which one — it's whether your child knows how to use any of them well and safely. Here's the comparison anyway, because you asked.

The short answer
  • No single winner. All three are capable general assistants and all set a 13+ minimum age.
  • Rough split: ChatGPT — most versatile all-rounder; Gemini — best tied into Google/search and often generous free access; Claude — strong, careful writing and explanation.
  • Safety depends more on setup and supervision than on brand. None is a "kids" product; under-13s should use child-focused tools.
  • The skill of using AI well matters far more than the tool. That's what a course teaches; a tool can't.

Which AI is best for kids? The short answer

For a supervised 13–16 year old, any of the three works. If you want one recommendation to reduce decision fatigue: start with whichever you already have access to. If you're picking fresh, Gemini is often the easiest because a family may already have Google accounts and generous free access; ChatGPT is the most flexible all-rounder; Claude tends to give careful, well-explained answers that suit studying. None of that outweighs supervision and a child who knows the rules.

Age limits for each AI

Age rule

All three require users to be at least 13 (and in some regions older, or with parental consent). They are not designed for young children. For under-13s, use kid-focused tools and treat these assistants as parent-operated.

Which is best for homework, creativity and coding?

  • Homework help (done honestly): any of them, if used as a coach. Claude's step-by-step style suits explanations; the way you use it matters more than the badge.
  • Creative projects (stories, art ideas): ChatGPT is a strong all-rounder for making things; pair with a kid-appropriate image tool set up by a parent.
  • Research: Gemini's search connection helps as a *starting* point — but every fact still needs checking, because all three can be confidently wrong.
  • First code / building: all can explain code; for a 10–16 year old, no-code building plus AI as the helper (our course's approach) beats diving into a coding language.

Are they safe for kids?

"Safe" isn't a property of the brand — it's a property of the setup. All three can produce inaccurate answers, all three learn nothing about protecting *your* child specifically, and none replaces supervision. The realistic safety picture:

  • They can be wrong with confidence. Teach your child to verify — see spotting fakes and false claims.
  • They're not private diaries. No personal details in prompts.
  • They're not companions or counsellors. For the risks of that category, read our guide to AI safety for children and companion apps.

The point most comparisons miss

Parents spend an evening picking the "safest" assistant, then hand it over with no rules. That's backwards. A child who understands how these tools work — that they predict text, agree by default, and sometimes invent facts — is safe on any of the three. A child who doesn't is at some risk on all of them. The tool is a footnote; the skill is the story.

Frequently asked questions

Is Gemini safe for kids?

Gemini sets a 13+ minimum and offers supervised/teen experiences in some regions. Like any general assistant it can be confidently wrong and isn't a kids' product, so it needs adult setup, agreed rules and verification of important answers. Under-13s should use kid-focused tools instead.

Which AI is best for students?

There's no universal winner. Gemini is convenient if you're in the Google ecosystem, ChatGPT is the most flexible all-rounder, and Claude gives careful step-by-step explanations that suit studying. Used well, all three help; used to copy, none do.

Is ChatGPT or Gemini better for homework?

Both work if used as a coach rather than an answer machine. Gemini leans research-and-search; ChatGPT leans versatile help and drafting. The habit — asking for explanations and checking them — matters more than the choice.

Can a 10-year-old use ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude?

Not on their own accounts — all set 13+ as a minimum. A 10-year-old should use these only with a parent operating the tool, or use kid-focused platforms designed for younger children.

Does it matter which AI my child learns on?

Barely. The transferable skill is knowing how to prompt, verify and stay safe — which carries across every tool and every future model. That's why we teach the skill, not the brand.
