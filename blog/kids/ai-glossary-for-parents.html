Jargon, Explained

AI Glossary for Parents: 30 Terms Every Parent Should Know (2026)

Prompt, LLM, hallucination, token, deepfake, agent. If your child's world runs on words you can't quite define, here they are — in plain English, grouped so you can find what you need.

SkillsUniversity Team • September 17, 2026 • 9 min read

You don't need to become an engineer to keep up with your child. You need about thirty words. This is a plain-English glossary of the AI terms that actually come up in family life — no maths, no jargon defining more jargon. Skim the essentials, then dip into the rest when a new word lands at the dinner table.

Bookmark it. It pairs well with our simpler explainer of AI, machine learning and robotics for kids.

The essentials (start here)

If you learn only these six, you'll follow almost any conversation:

TermIn plain English
AI (Artificial Intelligence)Software that does things we used to think needed human intelligence — like understanding language or recognising images. See our simple explainer.
Chatbot / AI assistantA program you type or talk to that answers in normal language (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude).
PromptWhat you type to an AI. A better prompt gets a better answer — the skill of prompt engineering.
LLM (Large Language Model)The engine behind chatbots. It predicts the next words based on patterns in huge amounts of text.
HallucinationWhen an AI states something false with total confidence. The reason we teach kids to check answers.
DeepfakeA fake image, video or voice made by AI to look real. Spotting them is a core skill — see our guide.

How AI is built

TermIn plain English
Machine learningThe main way AI 'learns' — from examples and data, rather than being told every rule.
ModelThe trained AI itself — the thing that gives answers. 'A model' = one specific AI.
Training dataThe huge pile of text, images or sound an AI learned from. Its strengths and biases come from here.
Neural networkA brain-inspired structure of connections the AI uses to spot patterns.
AlgorithmA set of steps a computer follows. AI uses many; the word just means 'the method.'
ParametersThe internal 'dials' a model tunes while learning. More isn't always better, but it's why models are called 'large.'
BiasWhen an AI's answers unfairly favour or ignore certain groups, usually because its training data did.

How you use AI

TermIn plain English
Prompt engineeringWriting clear instructions to get good results. A language skill, not coding.
TokenA chunk of text (roughly a word-piece) an AI reads and writes in. 'Context limit' is measured in tokens.
Context windowHow much an AI can 'remember' in one conversation before it starts forgetting the start.
Fine-tuningTaking a general model and training it a bit more for a specific job.
MultimodalAn AI that handles more than text — also images, audio or video.
AgentAn AI that doesn't just answer but takes actions (clicks, books, sends) toward a goal. Newer and worth watching.
Open source vs closedWhether a model's inner workings are public (open) or kept private by a company (closed).

Safety & risk terms

TermIn plain English
Companion appA chatbot designed to feel like a friend or partner. Higher-risk for kids — see our safety guide.
SycophancyAn AI's tendency to agree with you, even when it shouldn't. Why it can validate bad ideas.
MisinformationFalse information, which AI can produce (via hallucination) or spread at scale.
Data privacyWhat happens to what you type. Rule for kids: no personal details in prompts.
Content filterThe safety layer meant to block harmful outputs. Helpful, but never perfect.
JailbreakA trick to make an AI ignore its safety rules. Worth knowing the word exists.
COPPA / age gateRules and checks meant to keep young children off adult tools. Most assistants set 13+.

Creative AI terms

TermIn plain English
Generative AIAI that creates new things — text, images, music, video — rather than just sorting or predicting.
Text-to-imageType a description, get a picture (used in AI art tools).
Diffusion modelThe common method behind AI images — it 'denoises' random static into a picture.
Voice cloningCopying a specific person's voice with AI. Impressive and easily misused.
Prompt (art)The description you give an image tool; wording changes the result dramatically.
Watermark / provenanceSignals that mark content as AI-made, to help tell real from generated.

Frequently asked questions

What does LLM mean?

LLM stands for Large Language Model — the engine behind chatbots like ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude. It works by predicting likely next words based on patterns in a huge amount of text it was trained on. It doesn't 'know' facts the way a person does, which is why it can sometimes be confidently wrong.

What is an AI hallucination?

A hallucination is when an AI states something false as if it were true — an invented fact, source or quote. It happens because the model predicts plausible-sounding text rather than looking up verified facts. It's the main reason to teach kids to check AI answers.

What is a prompt in AI?

A prompt is simply what you type to an AI to get a response. The clarity of the prompt strongly affects the quality of the answer — which is why 'prompt engineering' (writing good prompts) is a genuinely useful skill for kids.

What's the difference between AI and machine learning?

AI is the broad goal — machines doing intelligent-seeming tasks. Machine learning is the main method used to get there — letting software learn patterns from examples and data. Most modern AI you meet is built with machine learning. Our AI/ML/robotics explainer covers this simply.

What is a companion app and why is it risky for kids?

A companion app is a chatbot designed to feel like a friend or partner rather than a tool. It's higher-risk for children because it's built to create emotional attachment and to agree with the user. Child-safety experts recommend against these for under-18s.
