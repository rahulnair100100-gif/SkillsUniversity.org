How to Talk to Your Kids About AI

Your child is already meeting AI — in homework apps, group chats and search results. The question isn't whether to have the conversation, but how to have it well. Here's a calm, practical way in.

SkillsUniversity Team • August 25, 2026 • 7 min read
The short answer

Lead with curiosity, not fear. Start from what your child already uses, keep it age-appropriate, and focus on a few durable messages — AI can be wrong, never share personal information, don't use it to cheat, and be sceptical of what you see. Make it an ongoing chat, not a one-time lecture.

Why this conversation matters now

Surveys suggest the vast majority of children have already used AI in some form. That means the choice isn't whether your child encounters it — it's whether they meet it with your guidance or figure it out alone from a friend or a video. A few good conversations now shape how they use it for years.

Start with curiosity, not a warning

Opening with "AI is dangerous" shuts the conversation down. Opening with "show me how you use it" opens it up. Ask what they've tried, what's fun about it, what's confused them. You'll learn a lot — and they'll actually listen when you add the guardrails.

What to say at each age

AgeThe main message
10–11"AI can be wrong, and it's not a person." Focus on curiosity and never sharing personal details. Sit with them while they use it.
12–13"There's a line between help and cheating." Talk about using AI to understand, checking its answers, and honesty at school.
14–16"Be sceptical of what you see." Deepfakes, bias, misinformation, and thinking critically about who made a piece of content and why.

The messages worth repeating

  • It can be confidently wrong — check anything important.
  • Never share personal information with a chatbot.
  • Use it to learn, not to cheat — the goal is understanding.
  • Not everything online is real — images, voices and videos can be faked.
  • Come to me if something feels off — no judgement.
For the parent who feels out of their depth

You don't need to be an AI expert to guide your child. Your job isn't to know every tool — it's to keep asking good questions and keep the door open. Curiosity from you teaches curiosity in them.

Easy conversation starters

Try: "What's the coolest thing you've made an AI do?" · "Has it ever told you something that turned out to be wrong?" · "How would you know if a video was fake?" · "Where's the line between AI helping with homework and doing it for you?" These invite real answers instead of shrugs.

The bottom line

Talking to your kids about AI isn't one big scary lecture — it's lots of small, curious conversations over time. Start from what they already do, keep the messages simple and repeat them, and stay approachable. That's what turns AI from something that happens to your child into something they handle wisely.
