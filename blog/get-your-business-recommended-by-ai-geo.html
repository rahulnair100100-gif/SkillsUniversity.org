AI Search (GEO)

Get Your Business Recommended by AI

Your customers are asking ChatGPT and Gemini what to buy. If AI isn't recommending you, you're invisible to a fast-growing slice of buyers.

SkillsUniversity Team • August 10, 2026 • 7 min read

Your customers have started asking ChatGPT and Gemini the questions they used to type into Google: "best [service] near me," "who should I hire for [job]," "what's a good tool for [task]." When AI answers, it names a few businesses — and if yours isn't one of them, you're invisible to a fast-growing slice of buyers. Getting recommended by AI has a name now: Generative Engine Optimization, or GEO. Here's how it works.

GEO is the new SEO — but not a replacement

For twenty years, being found meant ranking on Google. That still matters. But a growing number of people now ask an AI assistant instead of scrolling search results, and the AI gives them a short, confident shortlist. GEO is the practice of making your business the kind of thing AI recommends. The good news: it overlaps heavily with good SEO and good content — you're not starting from zero.

The core idea in one sentence

AI models recommend businesses they can clearly understand and find consistently described across the web. So the goal is to be legible to AI: clear about what you do, for whom, and consistently confirmed by multiple credible sources.

What actually influences AI recommendations

Based on how these systems work, a handful of things move the needle:

  • Clear, specific descriptions of what you do. "We do marketing" is invisible. "WhatsApp marketing automation for dental clinics in India" is something AI can match to a question.
  • Consistency across the web. Your name, service, and location described the same way on your site, directories, and profiles. AI trusts what's confirmed in multiple places.
  • Content that answers real questions. Articles that directly answer what customers ask are exactly what AI pulls from when composing a recommendation.
  • Reviews and third-party mentions. Being talked about elsewhere — reviews, listicles, mentions — signals that you're real and credible.
  • Structured, machine-readable information. Clear pages, FAQs, and schema markup help AI understand and quote you accurately.

A practical GEO starter plan

  1. Nail your one-sentence description. Exactly what you do, for whom, and where. Use it consistently everywhere.
  2. Publish question-answering content. Write clear articles answering the exact questions your customers ask AI — the kind you'd want to be quoted for.
  3. Clean up your consistency. Make sure your business is described identically across your site, Google Business Profile, and any directories.
  4. Earn honest reviews and mentions. Ask happy customers for reviews; get listed where your industry gets listed.
  5. Test it. Ask ChatGPT and Gemini the questions your customers would. See if you appear — and who does instead.

Don't abandon what works

GEO is additive, not a replacement. Google search, your website, and word of mouth still drive most business for most companies. Think of GEO as claiming your spot in a new, fast-growing channel early — while your competitors don't even know it exists yet. Early movers in any channel get outsized returns, and right now GEO is wide open.

The bottom line

As more customers ask AI instead of searching, being recommended by ChatGPT and Gemini becomes real distribution. The businesses that win aren't gaming a system — they're the ones that describe themselves clearly, answer real questions, stay consistent across the web, and earn genuine credibility. Do that, and you become exactly what an AI wants to recommend.
