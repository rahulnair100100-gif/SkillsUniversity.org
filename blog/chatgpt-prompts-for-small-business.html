ChatGPT Prompts

20 ChatGPT Prompts Every Business Owner Needs

Most owners give up on ChatGPT too early. The problem is never the tool — it's the prompt. Steal these patterns and keep the good ones.

SkillsUniversity Team • August 8, 2026 • 7 min read

Most small business owners who try ChatGPT give up too early. They ask a vague question, get a generic answer, and conclude it's overhyped. The truth is almost boring: the quality of what you get out depends entirely on what you put in. Below are prompt patterns — organized by job — that consistently produce useful results. Steal them, swap in your details, and keep the good ones.

The one habit that changes everything

Give ChatGPT a role, context, and a format. Not “write me a marketing email” but “You're an email copywriter for a small dental clinic. Write a friendly reminder email to patients overdue for a checkup. Keep it under 120 words, warm but professional.” Role + context + format is the difference between mediocre and genuinely useful.

Marketing & content

  • Content ideas: "Give me 10 blog post ideas for [business type] that my ideal customer ([describe them]) would actually search for on Google."
  • Repurpose: "Turn this blog post into 5 short social posts, each with a different hook. Here's the post: [paste]."
  • Ad copy: "Write 3 versions of a Facebook ad for [product], each targeting a different customer pain point. Under 40 words each."
  • Email subject lines: "Write 8 subject lines for an email about [topic]. Mix curiosity, urgency, and clarity. Keep them under 45 characters."

Sales & outreach

  • Cold outreach: "Write a short, non-pushy outreach message to [type of prospect]. Reference [specific detail]. Goal: book a 15-minute call."
  • Follow-up: "Draft a polite follow-up to a lead who went quiet after I sent a quote 5 days ago. Keep it light and low-pressure."
  • Objection handling: "A prospect says [objection]. Give me 3 honest, non-salesy ways to respond."

Customer service

  • Tone-fix: "Rewrite this reply to an upset customer so it's empathetic and professional without admitting fault: [paste]."
  • FAQ builder: "Here are my 10 most common customer questions and rough answers. Turn them into clear, friendly FAQ content: [paste]."
  • Templates: "Create 5 reusable reply templates for common [business type] enquiries I can personalize quickly."

Operations & admin

  • Find what to automate: "Here's a list of my weekly tasks. Rate each 1–10 on how easily AI could automate or speed it up, and explain why: [paste]."
  • SOPs: "Write a step-by-step standard operating procedure for [task] that a new hire could follow without asking questions."
  • Meeting notes: "Summarize these messy meeting notes into decisions made, action items with owners, and open questions: [paste]."

Finance & planning

  • Plain-English numbers: "Explain what these figures suggest about my business in simple terms, and what questions I should be asking: [paste]."
  • Pricing sanity-check: "I charge [price] for [service]. Walk me through the factors I should weigh before raising it."
  • Budget draft: "Help me outline a simple monthly budget for a [business type] doing roughly [revenue]. List typical cost categories."

Strategy & thinking

  • Pressure-test an idea: "I'm considering [idea]. Play devil's advocate and give me the 5 strongest reasons it might fail."
  • Differentiation: "Based on this description of my business, what makes me genuinely different from competitors — and how should I say it? [paste]."
  • Weekly planning: "Here are my goals and my task list. Help me prioritize this week for maximum impact: [paste]."
  • Decision helper: "I'm torn between [option A] and [option B]. Ask me 5 questions that would help me decide, then summarize the trade-offs."

Two rules to keep you out of trouble

First, never send AI output unread. It can be confidently wrong, invent facts, or miss your brand voice. You're the editor, always. Second, don't paste sensitive data — customer records, passwords, anything confidential — into a public AI tool. Treat it like a very capable freelancer you just met, not a vault.

The bottom line

These prompts aren't magic — the pattern behind them is. Give the AI a role, real context, and the exact format you want, then edit what comes back. Master that, and ChatGPT stops being a novelty and becomes the most flexible, tireless assistant your business has ever had.
